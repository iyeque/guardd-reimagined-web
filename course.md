[0:00-0:15] Hello and welcome. I'm [Your Name], founder of the Digital Guardian Wellness Initiative.

[0:15-0:35] We live in an age of constant connectivity. The average person spends over 7 hours daily on screens—that's nearly half our waking lives. But here is the truth: technology was designed to serve us, yet somewhere along the way, we started serving our devices.

[0:35-1:00] Our mission is simple. We empower parents, young adults, and educators with practical tools and a shared language to build healthy digital habits. No fear. No shame. Just actionable skills.

[1:00-1:20] So what exactly is digital wellness? It is the intentional, balanced, and sustainable approach to using technology. It is not about quitting screens entirely—it is about using them to enhance your life without letting them overwhelm your mental health, relationships, or focus.

[1:20-1:45] Think of digital wellness as a skill, not a rigid rule. It requires three things: awareness of your patterns, intention behind every tap and scroll, and the courage to pause before reacting.

[1:45-2:00] Over the next hour, we will journey from self-awareness to curation, then protection, and finally, future-ready digital literacy. Let us begin with the data that matters—your screen time.

M2: Checking Your Digital Wellbeing (Android & iOS) – 4:45
[0:00-0:15] You cannot change what you do not measure. Without data, we rely on guilt and guesswork. Both Android and iOS have powerful, built-in tools to give you the clear picture.

[0:15-0:45] Let us start with Android. Open Settings and scroll to Digital Wellbeing & Parental Controls. Your dashboard displays three critical metrics: Screen time (total hours), Unlocks (how often you reach for your phone), and Notifications (how often you are interrupted). Android also offers Focus mode to pause distracting apps during work or family time, and Bedtime mode which turns the screen grayscale and silences notifications to protect your sleep.

[0:45-1:15] On iOS, go to Settings > Screen Time. You will see a similar dashboard. Use Downtime to schedule breaks from specific apps, and App Limits to set daily caps for entire categories like Social Networking or Games. Parents, you can set a dedicated Screen Time passcode to lock these settings, ensuring your children cannot bypass them. Through Family Sharing, you can remotely view your kids' usage and approve extra time directly from your own device.

[1:15-1:45] But here is the nuance: Screen time alone is not the villain. Two hours on an educational app is vastly different from two hours on doom-scrolling news feeds. Pay attention to your pickup count. The average user unlocks their phone over 100 times a day. Each unlock fractures your attention, even if you only glance for a few seconds.

[1:45-2:15] For parents, iOS now provides an "at-a-glance" weekly report for your child's device, showing their average usage and the specific apps they spent the most time in. Android's Family Link offers similar supervision, allowing you to set daily limits and approve app downloads remotely.

[2:15-2:45] Remember, the goal of these tools is not to punish yourself or your children. It is to build awareness. Spend the first week simply observing. Notice your most-used app. Notice the time of day you pick up your phone the most. Is it boredom? Stress? Procrastination? Identify the trigger.

[2:45-3:15] Once you have identified your top trigger app, set a realistic limit. If you spend three hours on Instagram, dial it down to two hours. Then next week, down to one. Small, gradual changes build sustainable habits.

[3:15-3:45] A pro tip: Turn on grayscale mode. On Android, it is under Bedtime mode. On iOS, it is under Accessibility > Display & Text Size > Color Filters. Without color, those "red notification bubbles" and vibrant app icons lose their addictive visual pull.

[3:45-4:15] Finally, schedule a weekly "Screen Time Review." Open your dashboard every Sunday evening for five minutes. Ask yourself: "Did this tool serve my values this week?" If not, adjust your limits accordingly.

[4:15-4:45] [ACTION PAUSE – END OF MODULE] Pause the video now. Open your Digital Wellbeing or Screen Time settings. Explore your metrics. Take a screenshot of your most-used app and your pick-up count. Then, set one realistic app timer for the coming week. Resume when you are ready to lock down your privacy.

M3: Privacy & Permission Settings – 4:45
[0:00-0:15] Before we curate what we see, we must first lock down who can see us. Your digital identity is a treasure trove of data—and right now, it is probably leaking in more places than you realize.

[0:15-0:45] Let us start with the social platforms. On Instagram, navigate to Settings > Account > Privacy. The single most effective toggle is Private Account, which stops strangers from viewing your posts and stories. Next, go to Two-Factor Authentication and use an authenticator app like Google Authenticator or Authy—not SMS. SMS-based 2FA is vulnerable to SIM-swapping attacks. Finally, go to Ads > Ad Preferences and turn off "Ads based on partner data" to stop third-party sites from feeding your browsing history to Instagram.

[0:45-1:15] On Facebook, go to Settings & Privacy > Privacy Checkup. Set "Who can see your future posts?" to Friends Only. Turn off Face Recognition to prevent the platform from identifying you in photos uploaded by others. The most critical setting here is Off-Facebook Activity. Click "Manage Your Off-Facebook Activity" and turn off "Future Off-Facebook Activity". This stops websites using the Meta Pixel from sending your browsing data back to Facebook for ad targeting.

[1:15-1:45] On TikTok, go to Settings > Privacy. Review "Who can view my account," "Who can send me messages," and "Who can comment on my videos." For teens, TikTok defaults to restricted settings, but double-check them. Turn off "Allow others to find me" by syncing contacts or Facebook to reduce unwanted discovery. TikTok collects extensive data, including location and even biometric data from videos. Under "Personalization," you can manage how your data is used for ads.

[1:45-2:15] Now, let us move to device-level permissions. On iPhone, go to Settings > Privacy & Security. Here you will see a list of every permission: Camera, Microphone, Photos, Location, and Contacts. Audit these ruthlessly. Why does a simple flashlight app need access to your contacts? Tap into each service and revoke permissions that are not essential.

[2:15-2:45] On Android, navigate to Settings > Security & Privacy > Permission Manager. It works similarly. Pay special attention to Location. Many apps request "Precise Location" when they only need "Approximate Location." Deny precise location for delivery, weather, or games—it is rarely necessary for those.

[2:45-3:15] Next, tackle Ad Tracking. On iPhone, go to Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking and toggle off "Allow Apps to Request to Track." This stops apps from asking to share your device identifier (IDFA) with advertisers. On Android, go to Google Settings > Ads and toggle "Opt out of Ads Personalization."

[3:15-3:45] You should also clear your advertising identifiers periodically. On both platforms, you can reset your advertising ID in the same menu. This is like giving your device a digital shower—it erases the behavioral profile advertisers have built on you.

[3:45-4:15] Finally, review your browser privacy. In Chrome or Safari, use "Private Browsing" for sensitive searches. Disable third-party cookies. These cookies track your activity across different websites to build a shadow profile of your interests.

[4:15-4:45] [ACTION PAUSE – END OF MODULE] Pause the video now. Audit your top three social media apps and your device permission manager. Turn off Two-Factor Authentication via SMS and switch to an authenticator app. Disable "Off-Facebook Activity" and "Allow Apps to Request to Track." Resume when you are ready to control your algorithm.

M4: Content Filtering & Your Algorithm (TikTok & Instagram) – 4:45
[0:00-0:15] Your feed is not a mirror of reality—it is a reflection of your past taps, pauses, and scrolls. Every millisecond you linger on a video trains the algorithm to show you more of that exact same thing. The danger? Echo chambers, algorithmic rabbit holes, and exposure to radicalizing content.

[0:15-0:45] The good news is that you can hit the "reset" button. On Instagram, go to Settings > Content Preferences > Your Algorithm. Here, Instagram uses AI to generate a summary of topics currently influencing your feed. You will see sliders or buttons allowing you to tell Instagram to show you more or less of specific topics. This update applies to Feed, Reels, and Explore in real-time.

[0:45-1:15] Additionally, you can use the "Not Interested" button aggressively. When you see a Reel or a post you dislike, tap the three dots and select "Not Interested." This gives immediate feedback to the algorithm, and the effect is instantaneous.

[1:15-1:45] On TikTok, the tools are even more robust. Open Settings > Content Preferences. You will find three powerful features: Block Keywords (you can add up to 200 specific words or hashtags you never want to see), Manage Topics (use the slider to dial categories like "Food," "Gaming," or "AI-generated Content" up or down), and Refresh For You Page. Hit that "Refresh" button and TikTok completely wipes your past watch history, effectively resetting your feed to a blank slate.

[1:45-2:15] Why is this important? TikTok's algorithm is extremely powerful. If you watch a video about sadness, it might start feeding you more heavy content. If you watch a single video about a conspiracy theory, it may lead you down a rabbit hole. Refreshing your feed prevents these negative feedback loops.

[2:15-2:45] For parents, both Instagram and TikTok offer Restricted Mode. In TikTok, it is under Settings > Preferences > Restricted Mode. In Instagram, it is under Settings > Account > Sensitive Content Control. This filters out mature or sensitive posts.

[2:45-3:15] A critical piece of advice: Feed your algorithm intentionally. Watch the content you actually want to see all the way through. Like it. Comment on it. Share it. This signals to the AI that you are a quality viewer, and it will reward you by recommending similar, high-quality content instead of rage-bait or low-effort clickbait.

[3:15-3:45] Also, recognize that lingering is a vote. Even if you hate a video, if you watch it for 10 seconds, the algorithm registers your attention. If you see something you despise, scroll away immediately. Do not give it your time.

[3:45-4:15] Finally, consider the difference between your Following feed and your For You feed. On Instagram, you can switch to "Following" to see chronological posts from people you actually chose. On TikTok, the "Following" tab is much cleaner. Use these tabs when you want a break from algorithmic recommendations.

[4:15-4:45] [ACTION PAUSE – END OF MODULE] Pause the video now. Open Instagram and TikTok. On Instagram, use the "Your Algorithm" tool to adjust topics. On TikTok, refresh your For You page and block five keywords that trigger negative content. Resume when you are ready to clean up your subscriptions.

M5: Manage Subscriptions & Feed Cleanup – 4:45
[0:00-0:15] Resetting your algorithm is powerful, but it is undermined if you are still following hundreds of accounts that no longer bring you joy or value. Unfollowing is not rude—it is digital hygiene.

[0:15-0:45] Research suggests we can only maintain about 150 stable relationships, known as Dunbar's Number. Yet, the average social media user follows over 400 accounts. Most of these are strangers, brands, or old acquaintances who do not truly connect with your current life.

[0:45-1:15] On Instagram, navigate to your profile, tap "Following," and sort by "Least interacted with." This is a goldmine. These are accounts you follow but never like, comment on, or share. Unfollow them with zero guilt. Your feed will instantly feel more intimate and relevant.

[1:15-1:45] On TikTok, go to Profile > Following. Scroll through. Unlike Instagram, TikTok does not have a "least interacted" sort yet, so do a manual audit. Look at the accounts you follow. Do they still align with your interests? If not, click "Unfollow" or use the Mute button. Muting allows you to stay following them but removes their content from your feed entirely—useful for friends you want to keep but whose content you do not always want to see.

[1:45-2:15] On Facebook, the clutter is often the worst. Go to Settings > Tab Bar > Customize, and drag the Feeds tab to your top bar. Now, when you open Facebook, you will see a chronological feed of posts from your actual friends, rather than boosted viral content, suggested posts, or ads. This single change drastically reduces the "noise" on the platform.

[2:15-2:45] For YouTube, cleaning subscriptions is just as important. Go to the Subscriptions tab, click "Manage" and review all your subscribed channels. YouTube often suggests unsubscribing from channels you have not watched in months. Take that advice.

[2:45-3:15] A great habit is the Quarterly Audit. Put a reminder on your calendar for every three months to do a 15-minute subscription cleanup. Our lives change, our careers change, our hobbies change. Your social media feed should change right along with them.

[3:15-3:45] What about the "Mute" function on Instagram? It is a quieter alternative to unfollowing. If you tap the three dots on a post and select "Mute," you can hide posts and stories from that account without them knowing. This is perfect for people you cannot unfollow (like a boss or family member) but whose content does not resonate with you.

[3:45-4:15] Remember, every account you follow competes for a sliver of your mental bandwidth. The more irrelevant noise you allow in, the harder it is to see the content from people you actually love. Less is truly more.

[4:15-4:45] [ACTION PAUSE – END OF MODULE] Pause the video now. Sort your Instagram "Following" list by Least Interacted and unfollow at least 20 accounts. Review your TikTok Following list. Resume when you are ready for your first assessment.

M6: Approving Content (YouTube Kids & Kidio) – 4:45
[0:00-0:15] You have mastered your own digital world. Now, if you are a parent or educator, it is time to master the digital world of the children in your care. Let us start with the most popular platform for kids: YouTube.

[0:15-0:45] YouTube Kids is a standalone app designed for ages 3 to 12. It has three age-based presets: Preschool (4 and under), Younger (5-8), and Older (9-12). But these presets still rely heavily on AI filtering, which misses about 20-30% of inappropriate content. That is why the "Approved Content Only" mode is essential.

[0:45-1:15] Here is how to set it up: Open the YouTube Kids app, tap the lock icon in the bottom corner, enter your custom passcode, select your child's profile, tap Settings, and go to Content Settings. Toggle "Approved Content Only". This completely disables the search bar and the algorithmic recommendations. Your child can only watch videos from channels or videos you have explicitly hand-selected.

[1:15-1:45] What is Kidio (also known as Kidoz)? It is a kid-safe search engine and browser for ages 6-14. It uses Google-powered safe search with multi-layer filtering, creating a "walled garden." Unlike YouTube Kids, Kidio allows web browsing, but it only surfaces pre-approved, age-appropriate websites and videos. It is commonly pre-installed on children's tablets.

[1:45-2:15] For older kids who have outgrown YouTube Kids, you should set up a Supervised Google Account through Family Link. This allows them to use the main YouTube app but with guardrails. You can choose their content level: Explore (9+, limited to educational and family-friendly content), Explore More (13+, broader but still filtered), or Most of YouTube (13+, nearly full access but restricted from live chats and comments).

[2:15-2:45] A quick note on YouTube Restricted Mode. On the desktop or main mobile app, scroll to the bottom of any page and click "Restricted Mode." Toggle it on. This filters out mature content across all platforms, even for logged-out users.

[2:45-3:15] Parents, the most important habit is co-viewing. Despite having the best filters, nothing beats sitting with your child and watching a video together. This allows you to discuss the content, answer their questions, and spot any anomalies that AI might have missed.

[3:15-3:45] Also, review the watch history frequently. YouTube Kids allows you to see exactly what your child has watched. If you see a questionable video that slipped through, you can remove it from their history, and the AI will learn not to recommend similar content.

[3:45-4:15] For educators, if you are using YouTube in the classroom, always use the "Embed" feature to play videos directly within your lesson slides. This prevents the algorithm from showing recommended videos after the main video ends, which could be inappropriate for the classroom setting.

[4:15-4:45] [ACTION PAUSE – END OF MODULE] Pause the video now. If you have a child, open YouTube Kids and enable "Approved Content Only." If not, open Google Family Link on your phone and explore the supervised account setup. Resume when you are ready to learn about reporting and blocking.

M7: Report & Block on Social Media – 4:45
[0:00-0:15] Even with perfect privacy and curated feeds, you will eventually encounter harmful interactions—harassment, spam, or inappropriate content. Knowing how to use the safety tools is non-negotiable for your digital wellbeing.

[0:15-0:45] Let us start with Instagram. If you receive harassment in a direct message, Instagram now automatically filters spam and offensive messages into a separate "Requests" folder. To block a user, go to their profile, tap the three dots, and select Block. You can also choose to block their other existing accounts. For reporting, select Report, then choose the reason—bullying, harassment, intellectual property, or inappropriate content.

[0:45-1:15] Instagram's Restrict feature is a stealth tool. If you restrict someone, their comments on your posts will only be visible to themselves. You will not receive notifications from their messages, and they will not know they are restricted. This is much safer than blocking in some situations because it prevents the harasser from creating a new account out of retaliation.

[1:15-1:45] Use Hidden Words under Settings > Privacy > Comments. Enable the filter for offensive comments. You can also create a custom list of specific words or emojis you want hidden. When someone uses these words, the comment is automatically hidden and sent to a "Hidden" folder for your review.

[1:45-2:15] On TikTok, reporting is similar. Go to the user's profile, tap the share arrow, and select "Report." You can also report individual videos. TikTok is particularly good at detecting bullying in comments—it often prompts you with a pop-up asking if you want to filter negative comments when you open the comment section.

[2:15-2:45] For Facebook, the reporting structure covers posts, comments, and profiles. If you see misinformation, you can report it under "False Information." Facebook is legally obligated to review these reports under various international content moderation laws.

[2:45-3:15] A critical legal and safety tip: Take screenshots. Before you report or block someone, capture screenshots of the offending messages or posts. Platforms sometimes delete content that violates their policies, and you need the evidence to appeal if the platform's AI initially rejects your report.

[3:15-3:45] What about "Report Abuse" on YouTube? If you see a video that violates guidelines, click the three dots below the video and select "Report." You can classify it as spam, hate speech, bullying, or violence.

[3:45-4:15] Remember: You are not responsible for someone else's bad behavior. But you are 100% responsible for protecting your mental health and online peace. Do not hesitate to use these tools. Do not engage with trolls—report, block, and move on.

[4:15-4:45] [ACTION PAUSE – END OF MODULE] Pause the video now. Open your main social media app (Instagram, TikTok, or Facebook). Locate the Report, Block, and Restrict settings. Find the "Hidden Words" or "Comment Filter" menu. You do not have to report anyone—just know exactly where these buttons live. Resume when you are ready for the human side of safety.

M8: Conversation Starters on Cyberbullying – 4:45
[0:00-0:15] You now have all the technical tools—the privacy settings, the blockers, the filters. But technology alone cannot stop cyberbullying. The true defense is connection. Yet, research shows that nearly half of all young people experience cyberbullying, and most do not tell their parents or teachers.

[0:15-0:45] Why? Because they fear losing their devices. They fear retaliation from the bully. Or they simply believe that adults would not understand the digital world. Breaking that silence starts with the right conversation starters. Not lectures, not interrogations—just open, curious questions.

[0:45-1:15] Try these specific phrases: "I have noticed you have been spending a lot of time on your phone lately. What has been the best thing you have seen online today?" Or, "Has anyone ever sent you a message that made you feel uncomfortable, even if it was meant to be a joke?" Or, "What would you do if a friend of yours was being treated badly in a group chat?"

[1:15-1:45] For educators, integrate this into your classroom. Try a scenario-based approach: "Here is a screenshot of a mean comment. What would you do if you received this?" The goal is to teach them to be upstanders, not bystanders. Teach them the "Three C's": Create evidence (screenshot), Check with a trusted adult, and Collectively support the victim.

[1:45-2:15] For parents, share your own vulnerabilities. Say, "When I was your age, I had a hard time with a kid at school. It really hurt. I know it can be even harder online where everything is public. If that ever happens to you, you can always come to me—I will not judge you, and I will not take your phone away."

[2:15-2:45] Avoid the "digital detective" trap. Do not secretly read their messages without consent. Instead, establish a Family Tech Agreement where both you and your child agree on boundaries like sharing passwords with each other for safety. Mutual trust is far more effective than surveillance.

[2:45-3:15] For educators, use "Circle Time" or advisory periods to role-play realistic cyberbullying scenarios. Ask students to act out different roles: the bully, the victim, the bystander, and the upstander. This emotional rehearsal prepares them for real-world situations.

[3:15-3:45] Remember the golden rule of intervention: "Praise in public, coach in private." If you correct a child's online behavior in front of their peers, they will shut down. Talk to them one-on-one in a calm, private space.

[3:45-4:15] Also, teach them about digital permanence. Once a hurtful message or picture is sent, it can be screenshot and shared infinitely, even after it is deleted. Ask them: "Is this message worth the potential consequences three years from now?" This builds long-term critical thinking.

[4:15-4:45] [ACTION PAUSE – END OF MODULE] Pause the video now. Write down three conversation starters you will use this week. If you are an educator, write a classroom role-play scenario. Practice them out loud until they feel natural. Resume when you are ready for the future of digital literacy.

M9: Digital Literacy & AI – 4:45
[0:00-0:15] Artificial intelligence is no longer a futuristic concept—it is embedded in every platform we use. From the algorithm that serves your TikTok feed to the AI chatbots like ChatGPT and the deepfakes circulating on social media. Digital literacy today means understanding this invisible puppet master.

[0:15-0:45] Digital literacy in the AI era rests on three pillars: How AI works, How it influences you, and How to use it responsibly.

[0:45-1:15] First, how AI works. AI is trained on vast amounts of data. Every search, every like, every pause trains the model. It finds patterns in your behavior to predict what you will do next. It is not magic—it is machine learning, a form of statistical pattern matching. When a chatbot writes a response, it is predicting the most statistically likely sequence of words based on its training data.

[1:15-1:45] Second, how it influences you. Algorithms create filter bubbles. If you only click on one side of a political issue, the algorithm will stop showing you the other side entirely. This polarizes communities and reinforces echo chambers. AI can also manipulate your emotions. Sensational, rage-inducing, or shocking content gets the highest engagement, so the algorithm pushes it. That is why you see so much negativity online—it is engineered to keep you scrolling.

[1:45-2:15] Third, how to use it responsibly. Use AI tools to enhance your learning, such as summarizing complex documents or brainstorming ideas. But never trust AI blindly. AI hallucinates, meaning it confidently presents false information as fact. Always fact-check critical claims using reliable sources like news aggregators, library databases, or official government websites.

[2:15-2:45] Platforms now let you control AI-generated content in your feed. On TikTok, go to Settings > Content Preferences > Manage Topics. You will see a slider for "AI-generated Content." You can dial it down to see less of it. Instagram is also adding "AI labels" to posts that were generated using AI tools, helping you distinguish reality from fabrication.

[2:45-3:15] What about deepfakes? These are AI-generated videos or audio clips that make people appear to say or do things they never did. If you see a sensational video that seems too wild to be true, stop and look for distortions in the face, unnatural blinking, or mismatched lighting around the edges. Use reverse image search tools to verify the source.

[3:15-3:45] For educators, teaching AI literacy is now as essential as teaching math. Show students how to prompt AI critically. For example, instead of asking "Why is climate change happening?", teach them to ask "Summarize three leading scientific theories on climate change and provide sources." This moves them from passive consumers to active researchers.

[3:45-4:15] Finally, protect your own data. AI systems are only as good as the data they consume. Do not feed private information into public AI chatbots. Their training data is often saved and can be accessed later. Treat every interaction with AI as a public conversation.

[4:15-4:45] [ACTION PAUSE – END OF MODULE] Pause the video now. Identify three places AI influences your daily digital feed. On TikTok, adjust your AI-generated content slider. Fact-check one piece of news you saw this week using a critical lens. Resume when you are ready for your final assessment.

import subprocess

    }
    # ... repeat for M3-M9
]

def add_action_pause_overlay(video_path, timestamp, output_path):
    """Add '⏸️ ACTION PAUSE' overlay at specific timestamp"""
    filter_str = (
        f"[0:v]split[base][tmp];"
        f"[tmp]trim=start={timestamp}:end={timestamp+5},setpts=PTS-STARTPTS[pause];"
        f"[pause]drawbox=y=0:color=black@0.7:width=iw:height=120:t=fill,"
        f"drawtext=text='⏸️ ACTION PAUSE':fontcolor=white:fontsize=48:x=(w-text_w)/2:y=40[paused];"
        f"[base][paused]overlay=enable='between(t\\,{timestamp}\\,{timestamp+5})'[out]"
    )
    
    cmd = [
        "ffmpeg", "-y",
        "-i", video_path,
        "-filter_complex", filter_str,
        "-c:v", "libx264", "-preset", "fast", "-crf", "23",
        "-c:a", "copy",
        output_path
    ]
    subprocess.run(cmd, check=True)

def composite_final(module):
    """Full compositing pipeline for one module"""
    temp_path = f"course-videos/{module['id']}-temp.mp4"
    final_path = f"public/course-videos/{module['id']}.mp4"
    
    # Step 1: Add slide overlay
    slide_filter = (
        "[0:v][1:v]overlay=0:0[slided];"
        "[slided]drawtext=text='Guardd Digital Wellness':"
        "fontcolor=white@0.8:fontsize=24:x=40:y=40:"
        "fontfile=assets/fonts/Inter-Bold.ttf[branded]"
    )
    
    cmd = [
        "ffmpeg", "-y",
        "-i", module["raw_video"],
        "-i", module["slides"],
        "-filter_complex", slide_filter,
        "-c:v", "libx264", "-preset", "slow", "-crf", "18",
        "-c:a", "aac", "-b:a", "192k",
        "-movflags", "+faststart",
        temp_path
    ]
    subprocess.run(cmd, check=True)
    
    # Step 2: Add action pause overlays
    current = temp_path
    for i, pause_time in enumerate(module["action_pauses"]):
        out = f"course-videos/{module['id']}-pause{i}.mp4"
        add_action_pause_overlay(current, pause_time, out)
        current = out
    
    # Step 3: Add progress bar at bottom
    progress_filter = (
        f"[0:v]drawbox=y=ih-6:color=#4FD1C5:width=iw*({module['duration']}/4500):height=6:t=fill[progress]"
    )
    
    final_cmd = [
        "ffmpeg", "-y",
        "-i", current,
        "-filter_complex", progress_filter,
        "-c:v", "libx264", "-preset", "slow", "-crf", "18",
        "-c:a", "copy",
        final_path
    ]
    subprocess.run(final_cmd, check=True)
    
    Path(current).unlink(missing_ok=True)
    return final_path

export const CoursePlayer = () => {
  const [currentIndex, setCurrentIndex] = useState(0);
  const [isPlaying, setIsPlaying] = useState(false);
  const [showAssessment, setShowAssessment] = useState(false);
  const [isPausedForAction, setIsPausedForAction] = useState(false);
  const videoRef = useRef<HTMLVideoElement>(null);
  const { completeModule, isModuleComplete } = useWorkshopProgress();

  const currentModule = COURSE_MODULES[currentIndex];
  const progress = ((currentIndex) / COURSE_MODULES.length) * 100;

  // Auto-pause at action pause timestamps (simplified - you'd use actual timestamps)
  const handleTimeUpdate = () => {
    const video = videoRef.current;
    if (!video || !currentModule.hasActionPause) return;
    
    // Example: pause at 4:15 (255s) for M2
    const actionPauseTime = 255; // You'd map this per module
    if (Math.floor(video.currentTime) === actionPauseTime && !isPausedForAction) {
      video.pause();
      setIsPlaying(false);
      setIsPausedForAction(true);
    }
  };

  const handleNext = () => {
    completeModule('digital-guardian-course', currentModule.id);
    setIsPausedForAction(false);
    
    if (currentModule.hasAssessment) {
      setShowAssessment(true);
    } else if (currentIndex < COURSE_MODULES.length - 1) {
      setCurrentIndex(prev => prev + 1);
    }
  };

  const handleAssessmentComplete = (score: number) => {
    setShowAssessment(false);
    if (currentIndex < COURSE_MODULES.length - 1) {
      setCurrentIndex(prev => prev + 1);
    }
  };

  return (
    <div className="min-h-screen pt-24 pb-16 px-4 max-w-6xl mx-auto">
      {/* Course Header */}
      <motion.div className="text-center mb-8" initial={{ opacity: 0, y: 20 }} animate={{ opacity: 1, y: 0 }}>
        <div className="inline-flex items-center gap-2 px-4 py-2 rounded-full bg-[#4FD1C5]/10 text-[#2C7A7B] text-sm font-medium mb-4">
          <span>🎓</span>
          <span>Flagship Course</span>
        </div>
        <h1 className="text-4xl font-bold text-slate-800 dark:text-white mb-2">
          Digital Guardian Wellness Initiative
        </h1>
        <p className="text-slate-600 dark:text-slate-400">
          45 minutes • 9 modules • 2 assessments • 9 action pauses
        </p>
      </motion.div>

      {/* Progress */}
      <div className="mb-8">
        <div className="flex justify-between text-sm mb-2">
          <span className="text-slate-500">Module {currentIndex + 1} of {COURSE_MODULES.length}</span>
          <span className="font-medium text-[#4FD1C5]">{Math.round(progress)}%</span>
        </div>
        <div className="h-3 bg-slate-100 dark:bg-slate-700 rounded-full overflow-hidden">
          <motion.div className="h-full bg-gradient-to-r from-[#4FD1C5] to-[#FF9A8B]" animate={{ width: `${progress}%` }} />
        </div>
      </div>

      <div className="grid lg:grid-cols-3 gap-8">
        {/* Video Player */}
        <div className="lg:col-span-2">
          <div className="relative aspect-video rounded-3xl overflow-hidden bg-slate-900 shadow-2xl">
            {currentModule.hasAssessment ? (
              <div className="absolute inset-0 flex items-center justify-center bg-gradient-to-br from-[#4FD1C5]/20 to-[#FF9A8B]/20">
                <div className="text-center p-8">
                  <div className="text-6xl mb-4">🧠</div>
                  <h3 className="text-2xl font-bold text-white mb-2">{currentModule.title}</h3>
                  <p className="text-white/70 mb-6">Complete the assessment to continue</p>
                  <button 
                    onClick={() => setShowAssessment(true)}
                    className="px-8 py-3 rounded-full bg-white text-slate-900 font-bold hover:scale-105 transition-transform"
                  >
                    Start Assessment
                  </button>
                </div>
              </div>
            ) : (
              <>
                <video
                  ref={videoRef}
                  src={currentModule.videoUrl}
                  className="w-full h-full"
                  onTimeUpdate={handleTimeUpdate}
                  onPlay={() => setIsPlaying(true)}
                  onPause={() => setIsPlaying(false)}
                  onEnded={handleNext}
                />
                
                {/* Action Pause Overlay */}
                <AnimatePresence>
                  {isPausedForAction && (
                    <motion.div
                      initial={{ opacity: 0 }}
                      animate={{ opacity: 1 }}
                      exit={{ opacity: 0 }}
                      className="absolute inset-0 bg-black/70 backdrop-blur-sm flex items-center justify-center z-10"
                    >
                      <motion.div 
                        initial={{ scale: 0.8, y: 20 }}
                        animate={{ scale: 1, y: 0 }}
                        className="bg-white dark:bg-slate-800 p-8 rounded-3xl max-w-md text-center shadow-2xl"
                      >
                        <div className="text-5xl mb-4">⏸️</div>
                        <h3 className="text-xl font-bold mb-2 text-slate-800 dark:text-white">Action Pause</h3>
                        <p className="text-slate-600 dark:text-slate-400 mb-6">
                          Follow the instructions in the video, then resume when you're ready.
                        </p>
                        <motion.button
                          whileHover={{ scale: 1.05 }}
                          whileTap={{ scale: 0.95 }}
                          onClick={() => {
                            setIsPausedForAction(false);
                            videoRef.current?.play();
                          }}
                          className="px-8 py-3 rounded-full bg-[#4FD1C5] text-white font-bold shadow-lg"
                        >
                          I'm Ready to Resume →
                        </motion.button>
                      </motion.div>
                    </motion.div>
                  )}
                </AnimatePresence>
              </>
            )}
          </div>

          {/* Module Info */}
          <div className="mt-6 flex items-center justify-between">
            <div>
              <h2 className="text-xl font-bold text-slate-800 dark:text-white">{currentModule.title}</h2>
              <p className="text-sm text-slate-500">{currentModule.duration}</p>
            </div>
            {currentModule.hasActionPause && (
              <span className="px-3 py-1 rounded-full bg-[#4FD1C5]/10 text-[#2C7A7B] text-xs font-medium">
                ⏸️ Includes Action Pause
              </span>
            )}
          </div>
        </div>

        {/* Module Navigation Sidebar */}
        <div className="space-y-3">
          <h3 className="font-bold text-slate-800 dark:text-white mb-4">Course Journey</h3>
          {COURSE_MODULES.map((module, index) => (
            <motion.button
              key={module.id}
              onClick={() => {
                if (index <= currentIndex || isModuleComplete('digital-guardian-course', module.id)) {
                  setCurrentIndex(index);
                }
              }}
              className={`w-full flex items-center gap-3 p-3 rounded-xl text-left transition-all ${
                index === currentIndex
                  ? 'bg-[#4FD1C5]/10 border-2 border-[#4FD1C5]'
                  : isModuleComplete('digital-guardian-course', module.id)
                  ? 'bg-green-50 dark:bg-green-900/20 border-2 border-green-400'
                  : index < currentIndex
                  ? 'bg-slate-50 dark:bg-slate-800 border-2 border-slate-200 dark:border-slate-700'
                  : 'bg-white/50 dark:bg-slate-800/50 border-2 border-transparent opacity-50'
              }`}
              whileHover={index <= currentIndex ? { scale: 1.02 } : {}}
            >
              <div className={`w-8 h-8 rounded-full flex items-center justify-center text-sm font-bold flex-shrink-0 ${
                isModuleComplete('digital-guardian-course', module.id)
                  ? 'bg-green-400 text-white'
                  : index === currentIndex
                  ? 'bg-[#4FD1C5] text-white'
                  : 'bg-slate-200 dark:bg-slate-700 text-slate-500'
              }`}>
                {isModuleComplete('digital-guardian-course', module.id) ? '✓' : index + 1}
              </div>
              <div className="flex-1 min-w-0">
                <div className="text-sm font-medium text-slate-800 dark:text-white truncate">{module.title}</div>

              </div>

            </motion.button>

          {/* Completion Badge Preview */}
          <motion.div 
            className="mt-6 p-6 rounded-2xl bg-gradient-to-br from-[#4FD1C5]/10 to-[#FF9A8B]/10 border border-white/20 text-center"
            animate={{

        )}
      </AnimatePresence>
    </div>
  );
};

interface Props {
  moduleId: string;
  onComplete: (score: number) => void;
  onClose: () => void;
}

export const AssessmentModal = ({ moduleId, onComplete, onClose }: Props) => {
  const isAssessment1 = moduleId === 'a1';
  const questions = isAssessment1 ? ASSESSMENT_1_QUESTIONS : ASSESSMENT_2_QUESTIONS;
  const [currentQ, setCurrentQ] = useState(0);
  const [answers, setAnswers] = useState<number[]>([]);
  const [showResult, setShowResult] = useState(false);

  const handleAnswer = (index: number) => {
    const newAnswers = [...answers, index];
    setAnswers(newAnswers);
    
    if (newAnswers.length === questions.length) {
      // Calculate score
      const correct = newAnswers.filter((a, i) => a === questions[i].correct).length;
      const score = (correct / questions.length) * 100;
      setShowResult(true);
      setTimeout(() => onComplete(score), 3000);
    } else {
      setCurrentQ(prev => prev + 1);
    }
  };

  const q = questions[currentQ];
  const progress = ((currentQ) / questions.length) * 100;

  return (
    <motion.div
      initial={{ opacity: 0 }}
      animate={{ opacity: 1 }}
      exit={{ opacity: 0 }}
      className="fixed inset-0 bg-black/50 backdrop-blur-sm z-50 flex items-center justify-center p-4"
    >
      <motion.div
        initial={{ scale: 0.9, y: 20 }}
        animate={{ scale: 1, y: 0 }}
        className="bg-white dark:bg-slate-800 rounded-3xl p-8 max-w-2xl w-full shadow-2xl"
      >
        {!showResult ? (
          <>
            <div className="flex justify-between items-center mb-6">
              <h3 className="text-xl font-bold text-slate-800 dark:text-white">
                {isAssessment1 ? 'Assessment 1' : 'Assessment 2'}
              </h3>
              <span className="text-sm text-slate-500">
                {currentQ + 1} / {questions.length}
              </span>
            </div>
            
            <div className="h-2 bg-slate-100 rounded-full mb-6 overflow-hidden">
              <motion.div className="h-full bg-[#4FD1C5]" animate={{ width: `${progress}%` }} />
            </div>

            <div className="mb-8">
              <p className="text-lg text-slate-800 dark:text-white mb-6">{q.question}</p>
              <div className="space-y-3">
                {q.options.map((opt, i) => (
                  <motion.button
                    key={i}
                    onClick={() => handleAnswer(i)}
                    className="w-full p-4 rounded-xl border-2 border-slate-200 dark:border-slate-700 text-left hover:border-[#4FD1C5] transition-colors bg-white dark:bg-slate-800"
                    whileHover={{ scale: 1.02 }}
                    whileTap={{ scale: 0.98 }}
                  >
                    <span className="font-bold mr-3 text-slate-400">{String.fromCharCode(65 + i)}.</span>
                    <span className="text-slate-700 dark:text-slate-300">{opt}</span>
                  </motion.button>
                ))}
              </div>
            </div>
          </>
        ) : (
          <div className="text-center py-8">
            <motion.div
              initial={{ scale: 0 }}
              animate={{ scale: 1 }}
              className="text-6xl mb-4"
            >
              🎉
            </motion.div>
            <h3 className="text-2xl font-bold mb-2 text-slate-800 dark:text-white">
              Assessment Complete!
            </h3>
            <p className="text-slate-600 dark:text-slate-400">
              You scored {Math.round((answers.filter((a, i) => a === questions[i].correct).length / questions.length) * 100)}%
            </p>
            <p className="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-4">
              Redirecting to next module...
            </p>
          </div>
        )}
      </motion.div>
    </motion.div>
  );
};
